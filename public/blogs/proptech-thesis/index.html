The Market Map Nobody Built

How resale, primary sales and rentals moved from local brokers to portals to tech-led companies, with the numbers and my calls.

Manan Ranka · Bangalore · November 2026
My thesis. Resale, primary sales and rentals each moved through three waves. In wave 1, local brokers were the market. In wave 2, portals made discovery digital, paid for mainly by brokers and builders. In wave 3, tech-led companies take responsibility for the deal itself: pricing, verification, closing and the experience. The waves overlap rather than replace each other, and value is moving from owning the listing to owning the transaction.

Wave 1

Local brokers

Offline, relationship-led. The broker is the market.

ResaleNeighbourhood brokers match buyers and sellers through relationships. Brokerage runs about 1-3%. This market keeps growing: resale rose from 38% to 43% of registrations in 7 cities between FY19 and FY25.
Primary salesBuilders sell through their own sales office and a wide network of channel partners, paying 2-5% commission on volume slabs, only when a booking closes. Channel partners drive nearly 60% of Mumbai-region residential sales (CREDAI-MCHI, May 2026).
RentalsLocal brokers find and show homes and charge about one month's rent. Most good homes never come online and change hands through brokers and personal networks.
Wave 2 · from 2005

Online portals

Discovery goes digital. The broker still closes, and now pays the portal.

ResaleListings move online and buyers start their search digitally, but the broker still does the deal. Brokers fund the portals: about 60% of 99acres' revenue mix (FY24) and 61% of Housing.com's collections (FY26).e.g.99acres (2005), MagicBricks (2006), Housing.com (2012)
Primary salesBuilders become the portals' second payer, at about 30%. Even so, portals get only ₹1,000Cr of builders' distribution spend, against ₹34,000Cr for channel partners. Discovery moved online; selling did not.
RentalsRental listings go online. A second model grows alongside: owners and tenants pay the platform a subscription instead of paying a broker, passing ₹1,000Cr of revenue in FY26 (unaudited).e.g.NoBroker (2014)
Wave 3 · from 2021

Tech-led companies

A company takes responsibility for the deal and the experience.

ResaleOne company handles pricing, staging, legal checks and closing. Some buy homes and resell them; others run the sale through channel partners. Still small: a few thousand crore of disclosed GMV, against 2.33 lakh resale registrations a year in 7 cities.e.g.Ivy, Truva, HouseEazy (4,000-plus channel partners), OpenHouse
Primary salesDistribution gets organised and bigger: large advisory and brokerage firms aggregate channel partners and are heading to public markets. The buyer-facing pitch becomes zero brokerage, with the builder paying.e.g.Anarock (IPO filed), PropTiger, Housiey, Propsoch
RentalsA company runs the whole rental: it leases homes from owners, finds the tenant and manages the stay, with products that cut deposits to as little as one month. Very early: about 170 homes, with a 14-day average rental cycle.e.g.Helium (2025)
Across all three
Each wave sits on top of the last: brokers still close most deals, portals still drive discovery, and tech-led companies are adding ownership of the deal.
The waves overlap. They did not replace each other. In primary sales, wave 1 still moves most of the money. Not every wave 3 company works through brokers either: some buy homes outright, take exclusive seller mandates, or market themselves as broker-free. Wave start years are approximate.

Segments & Tools

How each segment makes money, where it sits in the waves, and who is in it. Company figures sit in Revenue (02); names link there.

1Resale homes

Market 2.33 lakh resale registrations a year in 7 cities, 43% of all registrations (FY25, Square Yards). No sourced estimate of value.

How money is made

Two models work: brokerage of 1-3%, or an exclusive seller mandate that earns a margin of at least 5% (my estimate). Some companies buy the home outright and earn the spread.

Where it sits

Wave 1 still closes most deals. Wave 3 is early and wins one dense city at a time: one company runs pricing, staging, legal checks and closing.

Portals here

Brokers pay most portal revenue: about 60% of 99acres' mix (FY24) and 61% of Housing.com's collections (FY26).

2Primary sales

Market Over ₹6L Cr of housing sales in the top 7 cities in 2025 (Anarock; 3.96 lakh units, down 14% by volume). A sales value, not platform revenue.

How money is made

Builders pay channel partners 2-5% on volume slabs, or hand a firm the whole project on an exclusive mandate for about 3-5% (Anarock's residential revenue is about 2.7% of the sales value it facilitates, my calculation). Buyer-side advice, still builder-paid, is the newer model.

Where it sits

Wave 1 dominates: builders spend ₹34,000Cr a year on channel partners. Wave 3 is distribution getting organised: large firms aggregating channel partners, now heading to public markets.

Portals here

Builders are the portals' second payer at about 30%, yet portals get only ₹1,000Cr of the ₹39,000Cr builders spend on distribution.

3Rentals

Market No reliable sourced estimate (IMARC's ~$2.8B for 2025 uses a narrow definition).

How money is made

Four models: a broker's one month's rent, a no-broker subscription, a managed furnished rental that keeps 20-45% of rent (Flent), and a long lease with guaranteed rent that keeps about 5-7% (Helium, my understanding).

Where it sits

Wave 3 is only starting: companies that lease or manage the home and run the whole rental, from tenant to deposit.

Portals here

Rental listings went online, and an owner-and-tenant subscription model grew alongside: NoBroker passed ₹1,000Cr of revenue in FY26 (unaudited).

Warzones: Live Model Fights

The open fight in each segment: who is competing, their edge and weak spot, and my call. What each model earns per deal is in Business Models (05).

01

Broker network, mandate or buying the home

Three ways to win the same resale deal. Which one takes a city?

Resale
Channel-partner networkHouseEazy

Edge Reach: partners already in the market bring sellers and buyers.

Weak spot Does not control the price or the home, so it is easy to copy.

Exclusive mandateTruva, OpenHouse

Edge Controls pricing, staging and closing, and every deal adds real price data. Truva also funds some inventory.

Weak spot Slow: it needs density in one micro-market before it works.

Buy and resellIvy Homes

Edge Certainty for the seller: an instant offer.

Weak spot Capital-heavy, and it carries the price risk if the market turns.

What decides it

Density in one micro-market, and who owns the seller relationship first.

Evidence so far
  • HouseEazy: ₹850Cr annualised GMV, 4,000-plus channel partners (company, Oct 2025)
  • Truva: ₹500Cr-plus of homes acquired, 6× YoY, $9M raised in Jan 2026
  • OpenHouse: about ₹10Cr of GMV a month (press-reported)
  • Ivy Homes: 850-plus homes sold (company); no round since 2022

Call Mandates have the strongest moat, buying outright is the most exposed in a downturn, and broker networks grow fastest. The likely city winner is a mandate player that uses channel partners to find buyers.

02

Channel partners vs exclusive mandates

Who controls new-home distribution?

Primary
Channel partnersLocal brokers, PropTiger, Square Yards

Edge Reach, and the builder pays only when a booking closes.

Weak spot No exclusivity, so they compete on commission slabs.

Exclusive project mandateAnarock

Edge Runs the whole launch: pricing, marketing and the sales team.

Weak spot Needs large builders, and each mandate ends after 9-24 months.

Buyer-side advicePropsoch, Housiey

Edge Buyer trust: advice or zero brokerage on the buyer's side.

Weak spot Still paid by the builder, so neutrality is hard to prove.

What decides it

Which firm the builder trusts with a whole launch.

Evidence so far
  • Builders spend ₹34,000Cr a year on channel partners (Aurum, company-reported)
  • Anarock: 450-plus exclusive mandates (company), ₹881.9Cr FY26 revenue, IPO filed
  • PropTiger: 816 transactions worth ₹838Cr in Q2 FY27
  • Propsoch: 1,500-plus families advised, $2M seed (Jun 2026)

Call Exclusive mandates win the large projects and channel partners keep the long tail. Anarock's IPO will be the first public price for the mandate model.

03

Who owns the tenant and the deposit

₹50,000Cr-plus sits in idle deposits (my estimate). Who builds on top of it?

Rentals
Managed furnished rentalFlent

Edge Design and experience that tenants pay more for.

Weak spot Needs high occupancy to cover its costs.

Lease and guaranteeHelium

Edge The owner gets guaranteed rent; the tenant pays as little as one month's deposit, with an NBFC covering the rest.

Weak spot Carries the vacancy and deposit risk itself.

No-broker platformNoBroker

Edge Scale, traffic and a society app (NoBrokerHood).

Weak spot Has not moved into deposits or managed homes.

What decides it

Who holds the deposit and the rent stream, because that relationship lasts 11-36 months.

Evidence so far
  • Flent: about 140 homes, 90-95% occupancy (company, Jan 2026), ₹21Cr raised
  • Helium: about 170 homes, 14-day average rental cycle, ₹5Cr raised (Apr 2026)
  • NoBroker: over ₹1,000Cr FY26 revenue (management, unaudited)

Call Helium is the earliest signal. The winner bundles deposit financing with rent-history credit scoring, landlord insurance and small-landlord working capital.

Predictions to 2030

Each prediction has a date and a test that would prove it wrong. The tag shows which wave it is about. All assume regulatory continuity.

Resale · By 2027W3

A mandate player passes ₹1,000Cr of yearly GMV in one city

Truva's own target is ₹1,000-1,500Cr of GMV within 12 months (company target, Jan 2026). Hitting it in one city would show the exclusive-mandate model can scale.

Wrong if: No resale mandate player reports ₹1,000Cr of yearly GMV in a single city by end-2027.
Resale · By 2029W3

Resale stays human-closed

AI prices homes, screens buyers and checks titles, but people still close ₹1Cr deals. Measured by whether any platform closes resale deals at scale with no human agent.

Wrong if: A platform closes a large share of its resale deals with no human agent.
Primary · By 2027W3

Two full-stack brokers list: Anarock and Square Yards

Anarock has filed its IPO papers (DRHP); Square Yards has talked of an IPO at $1.5-2B. Listings would give the first public price for primary-sales distribution.

Wrong if: Neither Anarock nor Square Yards has listed by 31 Dec 2027.
Rentals · By 2028W3

A managed-rental operator reaches 5,000 homes in one city

Asset-light operators that take the owner's home and run the whole rental scale city by city. Today Flent manages about 140 homes and Helium about 170 (company figures), so this is a high bar.

Wrong if: No managed-rental operator has 5,000 homes in a single city by end-2028.
Portals · By 2028W2

A standalone portal adds a transaction layer or gets sold

Housing.com already went to Aurum in 2026. Listings alone earn about ₹25-30K per ₹1Cr deal, so the remaining portals have to move closer to the transaction.

Wrong if: 99acres and MagicBricks are both still listings-only and independent at end-2028.
All three · By 2030W2+W3

Aurum is a materially larger company

With Housing.com, PropTiger, NestAway and an SM REIT, it becomes the roll-up winner if integration holds. Measured by revenue and profit after the Housing.com consolidation.

Wrong if: Housing.com or PropTiger is sold or written down, or Aurum turns loss-making for a full year.

Where I'd Build

Personal view, not investment advice. One build per segment: the gap, how to start, and how it fails. Models and ratings are as in Business Models (05) and the Scorecard (09).

Resale

A city-dense exclusive-mandate platform in Bangalore

Exclusive mandate · STRONG BUY
Seed/A ₹3-10Cr
The gap

HouseEazy leads NCR and Truva leads Mumbai. Bangalore has a funded iBuyer (Ivy Homes, $7M seed in 2022), and Truva has announced Bengaluru (Jan 2026), but no one has yet shown a dense mandate cluster there.

How to start

Win seller mandates in one micro-market.

More

An exclusive listing means you control pricing, staging, negotiation and documentation, and every closed mandate is real price data. At 300 mandates in one cluster you set the market price, not the portals. Channel partners bring buyers for a 40% referral cut (my assumption).

5%-plusmargin per mandate (my est.)
40-50%margin after the partner split (my est.)
300-plusmandates to set the price (my est.)
Failure mode: expanding before about 70% mandate capture in one micro-market (my threshold); scaling before density destroys the data moat.
Rentals

Managed homes in gated communities

Lease and guarantee or managed furnished · BUY
Seed
The gap

Good gated-community homes rarely come online, and deposits of 3-10 months' rent keep tenants out. Helium manages about 170 homes and Flent about 140 (company figures), so no one has scale yet.

How to start

Take exclusive control of homes in one society.

More

Guarantee the owner's rent, cut the tenant's deposit with an NBFC partner, and handle tenant sourcing, verification, collection and renewal.

Where next

Tier-2 cities such as Jaipur, Indore, Surat and Coimbatore have no organised supply.

14 daysaverage rental cycle (Helium)
60 homesin one society before the next (my rule of thumb)
Failure mode: spreading thin: 60 homes in one society beats 600 scattered across a city.

Primary sales Not yet. Revisit after Anarock's IPO prices the mandate model (Scorecard: HOLD and NOT RATED).

What I'd avoid Standalone portals, fixed leases and broker-only rentals. The Scorecard rates portals and rental brokers AVOID.
